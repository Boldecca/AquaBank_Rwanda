The Challenge

Kigali Has Water When It Rains.
But What Happens When It Doesn’t?

Kigali experiences heavy seasonal rainfall, yet dry-season shortages leave households and businesses without reliable water. Our analysis identifies an estimated 65,000 m³/day supply gap in Kigali’s water system.

🌵

Seasonal Water Shortages

Dry seasons bring acute water scarcity, forcing families and businesses to seek expensive or unsafe alternatives.

🌊

Rainwater Runoff Waste

Millions of litres of rainwater flow unused into drains every rainy season — a massive untapped resource.

🏙️

Growing Urban Demand

Kigali’s rapid urbanisation is outpacing water infrastructure, widening the gap between supply and demand.

Our Solution

Turning Water Abundance
Into Water Security

AquaBank Rwanda bridges Kigali’s seasonal water gap — capturing abundance during the wet season and delivering safe, tested water when it’s needed most.

01 — CAPTURE
🌧️

Capture

Rooftop gutters, surface channels, and permitted surplus treated-water intake points collect rainwater during Kigali’s wet seasons before it runs off unused.

→
02 — STORE
🏗️

Store

Water is held in sealed, certified storage tanks at secure sites across the city, buffering supply so households and businesses have access even in the dry season.

→
03 — FILTER / DISINFECT / TEST
🔬

Filter, Disinfect & Test

Stored water passes through filtration, disinfection, and quality testing — checking turbidity, pH and chlorine — before it is cleared for distribution.

→
04 — DISTRIBUTE
🚰

Distribute

Tested water is delivered via kiosks, scheduled trucks, and smart-metered connections — reaching households, businesses and institutions across Kigali.

Verified caution: the often-cited 65,000 m³/day supply gap should be treated as a planning assumption until verified against official WASAC, Rwanda Water Resources Board and Kigali municipal network data.

Verified facts

Kigali's demand is rising

Urban expansion, densification and rising household/commercial demand continue to place pressure on water access and service reliability in Kigali.

Research findings

Seasonality is the key challenge

Rainfall can be substantial in wetter periods, but the gap between rainy-season abundance and dry-season scarcity is a major urban water challenge.

Assumption

There is measurable unmet demand

AquaBank assumes a meaningful gap between reliable water supply and actual demand among households, businesses and institutions in selected Kigali zones.

Forecast

Storage and delivery creates value

Where supply is unreliable, predictable storage, quality testing and scheduled delivery become a commercially valuable service.

Target market

Customer personas for Kigali

🏠

Low/middle-income households

  • Problem: inconsistent access during dry periods.
  • Current solution: daily purchase, storage, or intermittent supply.
  • Need: around 100–500 L per day depending on family needs.
  • Why AquaBank: predictable delivery and transparent pricing.
💇

Salons

  • Problem: daily operations depend on reliable water.
  • Current solution: tanker purchases or storage tanks.
  • Need: 300–1,000 L daily.
  • Why AquaBank: dependable supply reduces service disruptions.
🍽️

Restaurants

  • Problem: water is essential for kitchen, cleaning and sanitation.
  • Current solution: informal bulk purchase and local storage.
  • Need: 500–2,000 L daily.
  • Why AquaBank: recurring supply supports health and operations.
🧺

Laundromats

  • Problem: very high load and interruption risk.
  • Current solution: emergency bulk buying or local storage.
  • Need: 1,000–5,000 L daily.
  • Why AquaBank: volume-based contracts fit this customer type.
🏫

Schools

  • Problem: water access supports sanitation and daily operations.
  • Current solution: public water, emergency storage or tanker supply.
  • Need: 1,000–10,000 L depending on size.
  • Why AquaBank: safer, traceable supply supports trust.
🏢

Apartment buildings

  • Problem: multiple residents share one unreliable supply.
  • Current solution: backup tanks or emergency bulk purchases.
  • Need: high and variable daily demand.
  • Why AquaBank: managed storage and delivery lowers outage risk.

Capture & movement

Gutters, rooftop channels, pipes, pumps and transfer lines move stored rainwater into containment.

Storage & safety

Secure tanks, overflows and lockable access points reduce contamination and system loss.

Treatment & testing

Filters, chlorination or UV disinfection and routine pH/turbidity checks keep the water safe.

Distribution

Smart meters, kiosks and delivery vehicles ensure traceable supply to paying customers.

Water safety

Quality and compliance framework

Confirmed requirements to verify

  • Applicable drinking-water standards and public-health rules.
  • Safe storage and treatment practices for harvested water.
  • Routine testing for turbidity, pH, chlorine residual and contamination indicators.
  • Documented maintenance and quality records before public sale.

What still needs authority confirmation

  • Exact licensing and permit requirements for a private water-distribution venture.
  • Rules around permitted surplus treated water use.
  • Laboratory testing frequency and reporting obligations.
  • Requirements for kiosks and mobile deliveries.
